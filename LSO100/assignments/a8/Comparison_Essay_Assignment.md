# Comparison Essay (10%)

## Purpose

The **Comparison Essay** is your opportunity to demonstrate the literary analysis skills you have developed throughout the course. Building on the proposal you submitted earlier in the semester, you will write a well-organized essay comparing **two Canadian short stories** studied in this course.

Rather than simply identifying similarities and differences, your essay should present a clear, arguable thesis and explain how the two stories develop a common theme, literary technique, or social issue. Your analysis should be supported by close reading of the texts and carefully selected textual evidence.

## Assignment Instructions

Write a literary comparison essay in which you develop a focused argument about **two stories** from the course.

Your essay should:

- Present a clear, specific, and arguable thesis.
- Compare how the two authors explore a shared theme, issue, or literary element.
- Support your ideas with relevant quotations and textual evidence from both stories.
- Analyze the significance of the evidence rather than summarizing the plots.
- Demonstrate thoughtful engagement with the texts and the literary concepts studied throughout the course.

Your comparison should emphasize **analysis**, not summary.

## Essay Structure

### Introduction

- Introduce both stories and authors.
- Provide brief context.
- Present a clear thesis that establishes the basis of your comparison.

### Body Paragraphs

Each paragraph should:

- Begin with a clear topic sentence.
- Compare both stories throughout the paragraph rather than discussing one story at a time.
- Include relevant quotations or specific textual examples.
- Explain how the evidence supports your argument.
- Connect each paragraph to your overall thesis.

### Conclusion

- Reinforce your thesis.
- Summarize the key insights from your comparison.
- Conclude with a thoughtful statement about what the comparison reveals about Canadian literature, the authors' perspectives, or the broader themes explored in the texts.

## Using Textual Evidence

Support your analysis with evidence from **both** stories.

When incorporating quotations:

- Introduce quotations smoothly.
- Explain the significance of each quotation.
- Avoid lengthy quotations.
- Ensure that your own analysis remains the primary focus of each paragraph.
- Balance your discussion of both stories throughout the essay.

## Formatting Requirements

- Include an informative title.
- Organize your essay into clear, well-developed paragraphs.
- Proofread carefully for grammar, punctuation, spelling, and clarity.
- Document all quotations and sources using the required citation style.
