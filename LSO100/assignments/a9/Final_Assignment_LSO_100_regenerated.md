LSO 100 – The Canadian Short Story

Final Assignment: Reading Canada Through Short Fiction

Weight: 25% Total: 100 points Due Date: Please refer to the Course Schedule and Blackboard for the submission deadline. Submission: Submit your completed assignment through the designated Blackboard submission folder.

Purpose

Over the semester, we have explored Canadian short fiction through a wide range of voices, communities, places, and experiences. Our readings have raised questions about identity and belonging, family and generational relationships, landscape and place, immigration, Indigenous experiences, gender, memory, loss, humour, community, cultural difference, and contemporary Canada.

We have also developed the skills needed to move beyond plot summary and read literature analytically. We have examined how writers create meaning through characterization, setting, symbolism, imagery, point of view, tone, language, structure, and other literary choices.

The Final Assignment asks you to bring these areas of learning together.

Rather than reviewing individual weeks separately, you will look across the course, make meaningful connections among the stories, and reflect on how your understanding of Canadian short fiction has developed.

Complete all three parts.

Part 1 – Looking Back: Your Journey Through Canadian Short Fiction

20 points | Approximately 300–400 words

Think back to the beginning of the semester and your understanding of what a “Canadian short story” could represent.

Explain how your understanding of Canadian short fiction has changed, expanded, or become more complex during this course.

Your response must:

explain what you now understand about Canadian short fiction that you did not fully recognize at the beginning of the course;

discuss at least three stories from different parts of the semester;

identify one specific example from each story that contributed to your developing understanding; and

explain what these different stories collectively reveal about the diversity of Canadian experiences.

Your reflection should be personal, but it must also demonstrate knowledge of the course texts. Avoid simply stating which stories you liked or disliked. Focus on what the readings helped you understand or reconsider.

Part 2 – Making Connections: What Do These Stories Say About Canada?

40 points | Approximately 650–750 words

Choose three short stories from different parts of the semester and develop a focused argument about one important idea that connects them.

Choose one of the following areas:

identity and belonging

home and displacement

family and generational relationships

landscape, place, and environment

immigration and multiculturalism

Indigenous identity, land, and colonialism

gender and social expectations

memory, grief, and loss

community and isolation

humour and cultural identity

change and contemporary Canada

Your response must:

Present a clear central argument about what the three stories reveal about your chosen topic.

Explain one meaningful connection among the three stories.

Analyze how each story approaches the topic differently.

Analyze at least two literary techniques, such as characterization, setting, symbolism, imagery, point of view, tone, irony, dialogue, or narrative structure.

Use specific textual evidence from all three stories.

Explain what we understand more clearly about Canadian experience when the three stories are considered together.

Do not write three separate summaries. Your discussion should place the stories in conversation with one another throughout your response.

Part 3 – Looking Forward: The Story That Should Stay

40 points | Approximately 650–750 words

Imagine that a future LSO 100 class could read only one of the short stories we studied this semester.

Choose the one story that you believe should remain on the course syllabus and make a case for why future students should read it.

Your response must:

Identify the story and explain why it deserves to remain on the syllabus.

Explain what important Canadian experience, issue, perspective, or question the story allows students to explore.

Analyze two literary techniques that contribute significantly to the story’s meaning.

Select one specific scene, passage, image, object, symbol, or moment that future students should pay particular attention to and explain its significance.

Connect your chosen story meaningfully to two other stories studied during the semester.

Explain what your chosen story helped you understand differently about Canadian literature or Canadian experience after completing this course.

Your choice is not being graded on whether it is the “best” story. Your grade will depend on how clearly and convincingly you support your choice through analysis, textual evidence, and connections to the course.

Course Text Requirements

Across the three parts, your assignment should demonstrate engagement with stories from different stages of the semester.

You may return to a story discussed in an earlier part when making a meaningful connection, but your complete assignment should demonstrate knowledge of a range of course readings rather than relying repeatedly on only one or two texts.

Use only stories assigned or studied in LSO 100 when making course connections.

Evidence and Documentation

Support your ideas with specific evidence from the stories.

You may use brief direct quotations or paraphrases where appropriate. Quotations should support your analysis rather than replace it.

Use APA-style in-text citations for quotations and paraphrases and include an APA References page listing the stories discussed in your assignment.

No additional academic research is required. The focus of this assignment is your own analysis of the course texts and your understanding of the course as a whole.

Length and Format

Your complete assignment should be approximately 1,600–1,900 words, excluding the References page.

Your submission must:

include all three parts;

use the headings provided in the assignment;

use complete sentences and paragraphs;

be double-spaced;

use a readable 12-point font;

include page numbers; and

include an APA References page.
